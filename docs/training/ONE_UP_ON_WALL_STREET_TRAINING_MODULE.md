# 📗 One Up on Wall Street Training Module (Peter Lynch)

## 1. Purpose
Operationalize Peter Lynch’s *One Up on Wall Street* (1989, rev. 2000) so analysts and AI agents can apply his “invest in what you know” framework with disciplined research, category awareness, and sell triggers.

Sources:
- Peter Lynch, *One Up on Wall Street*.
- Elearnmarkets book summary (retrieved Nov 16, 2025) covering chapter highlights and checklists.

---

## 2. Lynch Investing Philosophy
| Principle | Description | System Hook |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Invest in what you know** | Everyday observations can surface great ideas, but must be validated with fundamentals. | Intake forms require “edge origin” (personal experience, channel checks). No anonymous tips without validation.
| **Dumb money advantage** | Individual/alt-agent investors can act faster than institutions constrained by mandates. | Allow lighter governance for sub-$1B market cap coverage; capture observational data from users.
| **Ten-bagger mindset** | A single 10x offsets many small losers; focus on asymmetric upside. | Signal engine tags “potential ten-bagger” when TAM, growth rate, and reinvestment runway align.
| **Stock categories matter** | Slow growers, stalwarts, cyclicals, fast growers, turnarounds, asset plays each need bespoke metrics. | Each agent must set category and associated KPI checklist before issuing a rating.
| **Two-minute drill** | If you cannot explain thesis in 120 seconds, you don’t own it. | Require summary + key risks + “how it wins” field in every recommendation card.
| **Recheck the story** | Narratives evolve; monitor data vs original thesis. | Portfolio monitor schedules “story refresh” tasks with auto-prompts every quarter.

## 3. Stock Classification Framework
| Category | Traits | Primary Metrics | Common Mistakes |
|----------|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Slow Grower** | Mature, modest growth, high payout | Dividend safety, payout ratio, debt | Expecting rapid re-rating, ignoring payout risk. |
| **Stalwart** | Large, steady 10–12% growers | P/E vs history, buyback capacity | Selling too early on small drawdowns. |
| **Cyclical** | Commodity/industrial swings | Inventory, cycle stage, pricing power | Holding through downcycle, missing macro signals. |
| **Fast Grower** | 20%+ earnings growth | Unit economics, reinvestment runway | PAYING any price—must check PEG, competitive threats. |
| **Turnaround** | Distressed but fixable | Cash burn, restructuring progress | Confusing hope with plan, weak balance sheet. |
| **Asset Play** | Hidden assets (real estate, patents) | NAV gap, liquidation value | Ignoring catalysts to unlock value. |

System enforcement: category choice unlocks context-specific KPI templates, sell triggers, and stress scenarios.

## 4. Lynch Dogma for Signal Generation
1. **Scuttlebutt Intake**
   - Capture customer/field feedback (e.g., product quality, store traffic).
   - Map anecdote → measurable metric (same-store sales, churn, etc.).
2. **Avoid List Integration**
   - Risk engine raises alerts for: “hottest stock in hottest industry,” “next big thing” labels, single-customer dependency, whisper stocks, roll-up sprees.
3. **Earnings-Centric Valuation**
   - Always tie P/E to growth (PEG discipline).
   - Document credible pathways for earnings expansion (pricing, volume, cost leverage, new markets, acquisitions) and track progress.
4. **Two-Minute Drill Template**
   - What the company does (category).
   - Why it wins (edge + moat).
   - Growth drivers & earnings math.
   - Key risks (what breaks thesis).
5. **Continuous Fact Gathering**
   - Use customer data, supplier calls, filings, store visits.
   - Maintain “story file” summarizing each update.

## 5. Portfolio Construction & Sell Discipline
### Buy/Sell Timing Signals
- **Stalwart**: Sell if P/E stretches 40–50% above historical range or fundamentals deteriorate.
- **Cyclical**: Trim when inventory builds, pricing softens, or replacement demand saturates.
- **Fast Grower**: Exit when growth slows, competitive edge erodes, or valuation exceeds growth multiple.
- **Turnaround**: Sell after fix succeeds and valuation normalizes, or if liquidity risk spikes.
- **Asset Play**: Monetize once asset value recognized or catalyst fails.

### Risk Controls
- Position sizing scaled to conviction and category risk (fast growers smaller weight than stalwarts).
- Diversify across categories to balance cyclical drawdowns vs steady growers.
- “Mirror Test” gating: confirm emergency fund, debt, risk tolerance before aggressive bets.

---

## 6. Training Assignments
1. **Two-Minute Drill Practice**: Draft monologue for one company in each category.
2. **Category ID Quiz**: Given KPI snapshots, classify stock type and justify.
3. **Avoid List Audit**: Review existing portfolio for Lynch “avoid” red flags.
4. **Ten-Bagger Case Study**: Reverse engineer past multi-bagger, highlight when thesis was rechecked.
5. **Sell Trigger Simulation**: Use historical stalwart/cyclical data to practice exit calls.

## 8. Key Reminders for Agents & Users
- Everyday insight is only step one; back it with data.
- The best time to buy is when you’ve found solid merchandise at a good price, not when headlines cheer.
- Stay humble: even Lynch warns not to blindly copy experts (he may change his mind, be wrong, or your context differs).
- Keep re-examining the business story—facts change.
- Ten-baggers require patience, conviction, and continual monitoring.

> “The more right you are on one stock, the more wrong you can be on all the others and still win.” – Peter Lynch

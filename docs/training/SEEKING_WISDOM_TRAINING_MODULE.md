# 📙 Seeking Wisdom Training Module (Peter Bevelin)

## 1. Purpose
Infuse our research and risk processes with the multidisciplinary thinking, bias awareness, and decision-making discipline taught in Peter Bevelin’s *Seeking Wisdom: From Darwin to Munger*.

Sources:
- Peter Bevelin, *Seeking Wisdom: From Darwin to Munger* (3rd ed.).
- Sloww.co detailed summary (retrieved Nov 16, 2025) covering psychological misjudgments and thinking guidelines.

---

## 2. Core Philosophy
| Theme | Key Ideas | Application to Trading Framework |
|-------|-----------|-----------------------------------|
| **Evolutionary reasoning** | Understand behavior as adaptive; incentives drive actions. | Risk agent analyzes managerial incentives, survival pressures, capital allocation histories.
| **Bias catalog** | 28 psychological tendencies (association, incentives, envy, anchoring, social proof, etc.) distort judgment. | Each investment memo must include “Bias Watchlist” tagging dominant risks (e.g., reciprocity, authority). Alerts trigger when crowd behavior overlaps with known biases.
| **Multidisciplinary models** | No single discipline solves complex problems; integrate physics, math, biology, psychology. | Decision engine references cross-domain models (feedback loops, entropy, power laws) for scenario planning.
| **Simple, meaningful reasoning** | Clarify definitions, simplify to essence, use checklists, think through second-order effects. | All system recommendations require clarity on meaning (definitions, units) before acting on metrics.
| **Continuous learning & humility** | Analyze errors, reward truth-seeking, accept uncertainty. | Post-mortems stored in `/learning/` with bias analysis; agents rate confidence honestly.

## 3. Bias Mitigation Protocol
### 3.1 Mandatory Bias Scan (excerpt from 28 reasons)
1. **Incentive-caused bias**: Align research with true incentives (comp, career risk).
2. **Consistency/commitment bias**: Beware holding losers due to ego; risk agent flags theses untouched for >6 months.
3. **Social proof & authority**: Document independent reasoning before referencing experts.
4. **Reciprocity & liking**: Discount overly positive sell-side notes tied to relationships.
5. **Confirmation bias**: Require “disconfirming evidence” section in every thesis.
6. **Deprival syndrome**: Track emotional reaction to losses; avoid doubling down to recoup.
7. **Anchoring & contrast**: Stress test valuations vs multiple reference points.
8. **Availability & vividness**: Balance flashy headlines with base rates.
9. **Do-something syndrome**: Add friction (cool-off timers) before reacting to news.

### 3.2 Combined Bias Monitoring
- Dashboard aggregates biases triggered across portfolio to identify systemic vulnerabilities (e.g., herd risk, momentum chasing).
- Meeting agendas start with “bias check” referencing latest metrics.

## 4. Decision-Making Toolkit
1. **Models of Reality**
   - Build latticework across physics (inertia, compounding), biology (natural selection), psychology (reinforcement), economics (incentives).
   - For each major decision, list relevant models and note conflicts.
2. **Meaning & Definitions**
   - Clarify terms (e.g., “Moat,” “unit economics”), units, and time horizons before debating.
3. **Simplification**
   - Strip problems to key drivers (80/20). Use checklists to avoid missing obvious factors.
4. **Probability & Base Rates**
   - Force explicit probability statements; compare to historical base rates.
5. **Second-Order Thinking**
   - Evaluate knock-on effects (regulatory response, competitive retaliation, investor behavior).
6. **Feedback & Adaptation**
   - Log outcomes, review against predictions, adjust models accordingly.

## 6. Training Exercises
1. **Bias Tagging Workshop**: Review past trades, label biases at play, propose safeguards.
2. **Mental Models Drill**: For current high-conviction idea, list cross-disciplinary models used.
3. **Entropy & Second-Order Simulation**: Map how small changes (pricing, policy) cascade over time.
4. **Disconfirming Evidence Challenge**: Debate strongest arguments against top holdings.
5. **Post-Mortem Retro**: Run a failure case through the 28-bias checklist and document learnings.

---

## 7. Key Takeaways
- Great decisions require understanding human nature, incentives, and multiple disciplines.
- Checklists and deliberate pauses prevent knee-jerk “do something” reactions.
- Biases multiply: watch for clusters (e.g., social proof + authority + vividness).
- Wisdom grows from studying mistakes—log them rigorously.
- Always ask: *What’s the simplest explanation? What am I missing? What’s the base rate?*

> “The world is multidisciplinary… we need to understand and use the big ideas from all the important disciplines.” – Peter Bevelin

## 1. Why Commodities Matter for Enterprise Trading

1. **Macro Transmission:** Commodities are the first channel through which growth, inflation, and geopolitical shocks travel.  
2. **Sector Linkage:** Energy, metals, agri prices directly alter earnings trajectories for listed sectors (Energy, Metals, FMCG, Autos, Airlines, Chemicals, Fertilizers, Shipping).  
3. **Currency Impact:** FX pairs (USD/CAD, AUD/USD, USD/BRL) track commodity cycles; INR sensitivity to crude/gold influences Indian markets.  
4. **Inflation Expectations:** Commodity indices shape breakevens, central bank policy, and hence discount rates for equities.  
5. **Portfolio Diversification:** Commodities often outperform late-cycle when equities struggle, making rotation critical.

## 3. Macro Drivers & Feedback Loops

### 3.1 Demand Drivers
- **Global GDP & PMI:** ISM/PMI > 50 → industrial metal demand up.  
- **China Activity:** Property starts, credit impulses, infrastructure spend drive base metals and bulk commodities.  
- **OECD Consumption:** Energy demand tied to mobility (jet fuel), heating/cooling (natgas).  
- **Population & Dietary Shifts:** Protein consumption (soy, corn feed) rises with emerging market income.  

### 3.2 Supply Drivers
- **OPEC+/Non-OPEC policy:** Supply cuts/boosts move crude curves.  
- **Weather/Climate:** Hurricanes (Gulf crude), El Niño/La Niña (agri yields), freezing (natgas).  
- **Mining Capex:** Long lead times (5-10 yrs) create supply inelasticity for copper, lithium.  
- **Geopolitics:** Sanctions (Russia, Iran), war (Red Sea shipping, Black Sea grain) disrupt logistics.  

### 3.3 Cross-Asset Feedback
- **Inflation Link:** Commodity surges feed CPI → central bank hikes → risk-off equities.  
- **Currency Link:** Commodity exporters’ FX correlate with their main exports (AUD-iron ore, CAD-crude, BRL-agri).  
- **Rates & Carry:** High real yields pressure gold, but benefit USD → weigh on commodities priced in USD.  
- **Credit Spreads:** Commodity collapses widen HY spreads (US energy names), dragging broader markets.

### 5.2 Two-Year Themes (2026-27)

1. **Energy Transition Tug-of-War:** Oil demand plateaus post-2026, but supply discipline may keep Brent >$70; natural gas remains critical bridging fuel; carbon pricing broadens (Canada, EU, China).  
2. **Copper Supercycle Lite:** Massive electrification (EVs, grids, AI data centers) with under-invested mines = structural deficits; watch recycling ramp.  
3. **Battery Metals Volatility:** Lithium/cobalt supply catching up causes boom-bust cycles; long-term bullish but tactical oversupply risk.  
4. **Agri Climate Risk:** Frequency of El Niño/La Niña leads to higher volatility regime; investment in climate-resilient seeds, irrigation, indoor farming grows.  
5. **Gold as De-dollarization Hedge:** Central banks (BRICS, Middle East) diversify reserves; gold remains strategic asset even if real yields moderate.  
6. **Carbon Markets Expansion:** Voluntary carbon markets tighten; compliance schemes (EU ETS, China ETS, California) converge, pricing power shifts to low-emission producers.

## 7. Risk Management & Hedging Playbook

1. **Define Commodity Value-at-Risk (VAR):** For each portfolio, quantify P&L sensitivity to key commodities (e.g., +$10 Brent = ₹X crore cost).  
2. **Use Layered Hedges:** Combine physical contracts, futures, options (collars) for asymmetric protection.  
3. **Correlation Monitoring:** Track rolling correlations between equities and commodities; during stagflation, correlations turn positive (risk-off).  
4. **Inventory & Working Capital Planning:** Commodity swings tie up capital; integrate into treasury forecasts.  
5. **Event Calendars:** OPEC meetings, USDA WASDE reports, EIA storage data, COP climate summits, Chinese NPC – set alerts for agent actions.  
6. **Counterparty Diversification:** Broker/futures clearing risk grows during volatility; maintain multiple relationships.
